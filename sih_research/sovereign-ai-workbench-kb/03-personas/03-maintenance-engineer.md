# Maintenance and Reliability Engineer

## Role

The maintenance/reliability engineer aims to keep equipment available, safe and economically maintainable. The role covers both reactive failures and proactive reliability improvement.

Typical areas include:

- Rotating equipment
- Static equipment
- Electrical systems
- Instrumentation
- Utilities
- Pumps, compressors, turbines, furnaces and exchangers
- Maintenance strategies
- Spare parts
- Root-cause analysis
- Turnarounds

## Typical day

### Morning activities

- Review overnight breakdowns
- Review equipment availability and unit constraints
- Check open emergency work orders
- Review critical equipment alarms
- Attend production-maintenance coordination meetings
- Prioritise new work requests
- Check overdue preventive-maintenance tasks
- Review contractor progress
- Monitor spare-parts availability
- Examine vibration, oil-analysis or thermography alerts
- Review equipment returned to service

### Technical work

- Diagnose pump, compressor, turbine or motor problems
- Review vibration spectra and trends
- Analyse repeated failures
- Conduct RCA or facilitate a multidisciplinary RCA
- Determine whether a repair is temporary or permanent
- Update maintenance plans
- Evaluate alternate equipment or spares
- Review vendor service reports
- Prepare technical specifications
- Support procurement
- Plan turnaround jobs
- Verify closeout of reliability actions

## What is slow

- Searching historical work orders
- Linking equipment failures to process conditions
- Finding the correct spare-parts specification
- Comparing vendor repair reports
- Reading vibration graphs and attaching them to work orders
- Tracking action items from RCAs
- Reconciling equipment names between EAM, drawings and historian
- Checking whether a recurring failure already occurred elsewhere in the refinery
- Preparing turnaround work packs
- Updating schedules when materials or contractors are delayed
- Converting field notes into formal closeout reports
- Obtaining signatures from operations, maintenance, inspection and HSE

## Example

**Current task:** A critical pump trips repeatedly. The engineer searches EAM history, previous breakdown reports, vibration data, seal-failure records, alignment reports, operating conditions, spare-parts history, vendor recommendations and similar pumps in other units. The first-pass investigation may consume half a day or more, especially if reports are scanned PDFs or stored in different folders.

**AI-assisted task:** The engineer asks for a five-year failure history for the pump and comparable pumps, correlated with suction pressure, flow, vibration, seal changes, operating campaigns and work orders. The system generates a timeline, failure-mode Pareto chart, likely recurring causes and a draft RCA template with source links.

The AI should not automatically declare root cause. It should show hypotheses and evidence gaps.

## Persona table

| Attribute | Typical profile |
|---|---|
| Technical comfort | High with EAM and equipment data; moderate with advanced analytics |
| Primary device | Desktop/laptop; mobile or tablet in field and workshops |
| Location | Maintenance office, field, workshop, control room and turnaround site |
| Shift pattern | Mainly day shift with callout or extended coverage for breakdowns |
| AI willingness | High if it can search equipment history and save report-preparation time |
| Main concern | False diagnosis, incomplete maintenance history and unsafe recommendations |
| Best interface | Equipment timeline, work-order search, trend viewer, RCA assistant and turnaround dashboard |
