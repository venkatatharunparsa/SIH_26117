# Inspection and Integrity Engineer

## Role

The inspection/integrity engineer ensures that pressure vessels, piping, tanks, heat exchangers, boilers, pipelines and other static assets remain safe and fit for service.

The role combines:

- Inspection planning
- Corrosion engineering
- Risk-based inspection
- NDT review
- Thickness monitoring
- Damage-mechanism assessment
- Fitness-for-service
- Statutory inspection
- Turnaround scope definition
- Repair and replacement recommendations

## Typical day

### Morning review

- Equipment due for inspection
- Overdue inspection tasks
- New thickness readings
- NDT reports received from contractors
- Corrosion-rate alerts
- Equipment operating outside integrity operating windows
- Leaks, seepage, vibration or process deviations
- Temporary repairs and clamps
- Statutory-certification deadlines
- Turnaround inspection scope
- Open recommendations from previous inspections

### Field and technical activities

- Visit equipment in the field
- Verify equipment tags and inspection locations
- Witness thickness measurement or NDT
- Review scaffold and access arrangements
- Check insulation removal and reinstatement
- Inspect tanks, vessels, piping circuits or exchanger bundles
- Meet operations and maintenance regarding access, isolation and permits
- Review contractor reports
- Evaluate corrosion or cracking indications
- Update RBI or integrity-management records
- Recommend continued operation, monitoring, repair, derating or shutdown
- Participate in MOC, HAZOP, incident or failure investigations

## RBI and corrosion-loop workflow

1. Define equipment or piping circuit
2. Identify process service and damage mechanisms
3. Collect design and operating data
4. Review materials of construction
5. Review historical inspection and thickness data
6. Estimate corrosion or degradation rate
7. Calculate probability and consequence of failure
8. Assign risk ranking
9. Define inspection method, coverage and interval
10. Generate inspection work scope
11. Receive and validate field results
12. Update the RBI model
13. Determine remaining life or fitness for service
14. Issue recommendations and due dates
15. Close the loop through EAM or inspection-management records

## Corrosion-rate calculation

Long-term rate:




CR_LT = (t_previous − t_current) / (d_current − d_previous)


Short-term rate:
CR_ST = (t_previous − t_current) / Δt




The engineer checks:

- Whether the measurements are from the same location
- Whether instrument uncertainty explains the difference
- Whether the rate is physically plausible
- Whether the damage mechanism changed
- Whether process conditions changed
- Whether a local pit or general thinning is involved
- Whether the calculated rate should be conservatively adjusted

## Approval chain (industry-based)

1. Inspector or contractor records field observations
2. Inspection engineer validates the report and data quality
3. Integrity or corrosion engineer performs the technical assessment
4. Operations confirms current and proposed operating conditions
5. Maintenance or projects confirms repair feasibility
6. HSE/process safety reviews risk implications where relevant
7. Head of inspection/integrity or engineering approves the recommendation
8. Plant or refinery leadership approves continued operation, major repair, derating or shutdown
9. Statutory authority or authorised inspector is involved where legally required

Any AI tool must clearly distinguish between **draft technical assessment**, **reviewed assessment** and **approved fitness-for-service decision**.

## What is manual and slow

- Locating the correct historical inspection report
- Matching inspection locations across old drawings and current equipment tags
- Transcribing thickness values from scanned reports
- Checking whether a report uses the latest revision of the isometric
- Converting contractor NDT PDFs into structured data
- Calculating corrosion rates from inconsistent measurement points
- Resolving unit, date and equipment-tag mismatches
- Rebuilding inspection history when equipment numbering changes
- Preparing turnaround inspection scopes from multiple sources
- Writing repetitive recommendations
- Checking overdue actions and statutory certificates
- Creating charts of thickness loss and remaining life
- Confirming whether operating changes invalidate an RBI assessment

## Example

**Current task:** Review a 150-page exchanger inspection package. Verify the equipment tag, match tube-sheet/channel/shell drawings, review thickness readings, read NDT contractor notes, compare current and previous readings, check process history, calculate corrosion rate, confirm minimum allowable thickness, determine whether repair or continued service is acceptable, and create a recommendation note.

**AI-assisted task:** The workbench extracts inspection points, aligns them with historical readings, identifies missing or implausible values, shows corrosion trends, retrieves the relevant design basis and produces a draft inspection recommendation. The engineer validates the data and signs the final assessment.
