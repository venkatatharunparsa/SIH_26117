# MOC Review Checklist

Purpose: controls temporary, permanent, organisational and procedural changes that are not replacement-in-kind. Typical length: 5–15 pages excluding revised drawings; complex change may create 50–200 page package.

## Structure

### Cover and change identification

MOC number, change title, unit/area, initiator, change owner, temporary/permanent/emergency, start date, planned completion/expiry, related project or work order.

### 1.0 Proposed change

Existing condition, proposed condition, equipment and tags, scope boundaries, temporary measures, what is not changing. Include marked-up P&ID or drawing.

### 2.0 Reason for change

Reliability improvement, regulatory requirement, obsolescence, capacity increase, safety improvement, product-quality improvement, vendor substitution, temporary bypass.

### 3.0 Technical basis

Design calculations, datasheets, material compatibility, hydraulic or process calculation, electrical load, instrument and control philosophy, relief and flare impact, utility impact, vendor information.

### 4.0 Affected documents

| Document | Current revision | Change required | Owner | Due date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P&ID DHT-204 | Rev. 6 | Update valve and bypass | Process | 25-Sep |
| Operating procedure OP-DHT-04 | Rev. 3 | Add startup step | Operations | 30-Sep |
| Valve datasheet V-204 | Rev. 2 | Update material | Mechanical | 25-Sep |

Documents commonly include: P&IDs, PFDs, isometrics, datasheets, cause-and-effect diagrams, control narratives, operating procedures, emergency procedures, maintenance plans, inspection plans, spare-parts records, training material, fire-and-gas drawings, HAZOP/LOPA/SIL records.

### 5.0 HAZOP and process-safety implications

Whether a HAZOP revalidation is required, affected node, new deviations, existing safeguards, new safeguards, LOPA/SIL impact, alarm/interlock impact, relief-system impact, human-factor implications, simultaneous-operation risks.

### 6.0 Risk assessment

Hazard, cause, consequence, existing safeguards, additional safeguards, initial risk, residual risk, action owner.

### 7.0 Required reviewers

Operations, process, mechanical, electrical, instrumentation, inspection, maintenance, HSE/process safety, environment, fire, projects, IT/OT cybersecurity, laboratory, legal/regulatory.

### 8.0 Required actions

Drawing updates, procedure changes, training, spare parts, inspection plan, maintenance strategy, permit requirements, regulatory notification, emergency-response update, software/control-system change, cybersecurity review.

### 9.0 PSSR checklist

Construction complete, equipment installed as designed, materials verified, pressure tests complete, instruments calibrated, alarms/interlocks tested, relief protection available, drawings updated, procedures approved, operators trained, maintenance and inspection plans updated, punch-list items risk-assessed, emergency response reviewed, permits and isolations complete.

### 10.0 Approval

Initiator, technical authority, operations, maintenance, inspection/integrity, HSE/process safety, project manager, unit head, refinery head or competent authority.

### 11.0 Closeout

All actions complete, documents revised, training complete, PSSR complete, temporary change removed or converted to permanent MOC, monitoring period complete, residual risk accepted.

## Good vs. bad

Good: defines change boundary, identifies affected documents systematically, checks HAZOP and safeguards, assigns reviewers and dates, has expiry date for temporary changes, includes PSSR and closeout.

Bad: treats change as replacement-in-kind without checking, updates equipment but not procedures or training, approves before drawings are revised, omits OT/cyber impact for control-system changes, leaves temporary bypasses open indefinitely.
