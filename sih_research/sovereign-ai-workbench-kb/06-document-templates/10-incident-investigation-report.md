# Incident Investigation Report

Purpose: formal HSE/process-safety record of an incident, near miss or major event. Preliminary report 2–5 pages; full report commonly 20–100 pages with evidence attachments.

OISD requires an internal investigation report for major onsite incidents within one month.

## Structure

### Cover and classification

Incident number, date/time, site/unit/location, incident classification, report revision, investigation committee, confidentiality, distribution list.

### 1.0 Executive summary

What happened, consequence, injuries/fatalities, release/fire/environmental impact, immediate containment, root cause, main corrective action.

### 2.0 Incident description

Exact location, equipment, work activity, materials involved, people involved, weather/environmental conditions, operating mode, permit and isolation status.

### 3.0 Consequences

Separate: people, process safety, asset damage, production, environment, public/community, financial, regulatory.

### 4.0 Chronology

Time-stamped table based on: DCS, historian, logbooks, CCTV, permit records, witness statements, phone/radio logs, EAM, emergency-response records.

### 5.0 Immediate response

Alarm, shutdown, isolation, fire response, medical response, evacuation, emergency communication, regulatory notification, area restoration.

### 6.0 Evidence examined

P&IDs, operating procedures, work permits, LOTO records, JSA, toolbox talk, DCS trends, alarm logs, CCTV, equipment history, inspection records, maintenance work orders, MOC, training and competency, contractor records, photographs, samples, witness statements.

### 7.0 Immediate cause

Example: Hydrocarbon release occurred after the flange was opened before positive isolation was verified.

### 8.0 Root cause

Potential categories: equipment/design, procedure, human factors, supervision, training, permit system, isolation management, maintenance quality, management of change, organisational control.

### 9.0 Contributing factors

Examples: incomplete line-up, conflicting drawings, poor communication, time pressure, inadequate gas testing, contractor unfamiliarity, alarm not available, temporary modification not documented.

### 10.0 Barrier analysis

Show: intended barrier, condition, whether it worked, failure mode, evidence, required improvement.

### 11.0 Corrective and preventive actions

Specific and verified.

### 12.0 Lessons learned

What should be communicated, which units are similar, which procedures need revision, whether fleet-wide inspection is required, whether training or audit is required.

### 13.0 Approvals and submission

Investigation committee, HSE head, operations head, refinery head, senior management, statutory/regulatory submission (where applicable), OISD submission reference (where applicable).

### 14.0 Attachments

Photos, drawings, DCS trends, CCTV stills, permits, LOTO, JSA, statements, inspection and laboratory reports, medical/environmental reports, action tracker.

## Good vs. bad

Good: protects evidence integrity, uses independent sources, separates facts/analysis/opinions, identifies failed barriers, avoids premature blame, provides verified actions, preserves witness and personal information appropriately.

Bad: begins with conclusion before examining evidence, blames an operator without analysing system conditions, omits permit/LOTO and MOC records, does not reconcile timestamps, closes actions without verification, circulates sensitive personal or legal information broadly.
