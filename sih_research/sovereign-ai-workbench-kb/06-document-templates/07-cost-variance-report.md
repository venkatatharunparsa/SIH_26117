# Monthly Maintenance Cost Variance Report

Purpose: explains why actual maintenance expenditure differs from budget and what management should do. Commonly an Excel workbook with a 2–5 page Word or PowerPoint summary.

## Structure

### Cover and reporting basis

Month, department, cost centres, reporting currency, accrual basis, budget version, prepared/reviewed/approved fields.

### 1.0 Executive summary

Total budget, actual, variance, forecast, main reasons, required management action.

### 2.0 Budget versus actual

| Cost centre | Budget | Actual | Variance | Variance % | Forecast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

### 3.0 Top cost drivers

Rank: major breakdowns, turnaround work, contractor expenditure, spare parts, emergency repairs, inspection and statutory work, projects incorrectly posted to maintenance, price escalation, foreign exchange.

### 4.0 Unit and cost-centre breakdown

Refinery unit, department, cost element, equipment class, operating vs. capital expenditure, planned vs. unplanned work.

### 5.0 Open commitments

Purchase order, vendor, work order, committed amount, invoiced amount, balance, expected month, accrual requirement.

### 6.0 Forecast

Full-year budget, actual-to-date, remaining plan, known upcoming work, turnaround spend, risk allowance, revised forecast.

### 7.0 Variance explanations

Each material variance should answer: What happened? Why? Was it planned? One-off or recurring? Financial impact? Does forecast change? Who is responsible?

### 8.0 Corrective actions

| Action | Owner | Due date | Expected impact | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 9.0 Finance review and approval

Finance analyst, finance manager, maintenance head, budget controller, CFO/finance head, senior management.

### 10.0 Attachments

ERP extract, work-order report, PO/GRN/invoice reconciliation, budget approval, turnaround report, accrual calculation, prior-month comparison.

## Good vs. bad

Good: states budget version, reconciles ERP/EAM and procurement data, explains material variances, separates timing from overspend, includes commitments and forecast, preserves source transaction references.

Bad: shows unexplained "miscellaneous" variance, mixes capex and opex, uses actuals without accruals, counts open purchase orders as actual spend, provides percentages without absolute values, includes commentary not supported by transactions.
