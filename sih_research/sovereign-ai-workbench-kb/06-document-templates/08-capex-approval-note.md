# Capital-Expenditure Approval Note

Purpose: a decision document, not a technical report. Tells the competent authority what to approve, why, how much it costs and what evidence supports the request. Typical length: 5–15 pages excluding annexures; large projects may require a detailed project report of hundreds of pages.

## Structure

### Cover and routing

Approval note number, department, project title, cost, budget head, approval authority, date, confidentiality, prepared/reviewed/concurred/approved fields.

### 1.0 Decision requested

State exactly what approval is requested:

> Approval is requested for capital expenditure of ₹25 crore for replacement of compressor K-204A, including procurement, installation, commissioning and associated modifications.

### 2.0 Background

Existing asset, age, service, previous modifications, current operating role, previous approvals.

### 3.0 Why the project is needed

Failures, obsolescence, capacity constraint, safety or integrity concern, product-quality impact, regulatory requirement, energy impact, reliability gap.

### 4.0 Consequence of not approving

Quantify: production loss, safety risk, environmental exposure, emergency-maintenance cost, customer or dispatch impact, future cost escalation, regulatory consequence.

### 5.0 Alternatives considered

| Option | Cost | Benefit | Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Repair existing | ₹6 crore | Short-term restoration | Repeat failure | Not preferred |
| Replace compressor | ₹25 crore | Reliability and capacity | Procurement lead time | Preferred |
| Add standby unit | ₹40 crore | Higher redundancy | Higher capex | Deferred |

### 6.0 Technical basis

Equipment history, inspection findings, process impact, design basis, technical specification, reliability analysis, scope of supply, installation requirements, commissioning requirements.

### 7.0 Financial basis

Capital cost, taxes and duties, installation, engineering, contingency, IDC (where applicable), operating-cost impact, avoided-loss calculation, payback, NPV/IRR (where required), sensitivity analysis.

### 8.0 Budget source

Approved annual capex, budget head, available balance, reallocation required, funding source, cash-flow impact.

### 9.0 Procurement route and recommended vendor

Open tender, limited tender, nomination or rate contract; justification; technical qualification; tender status; recommended vendor; price reasonableness; negotiation outcome; conflict-of-interest declaration.

The recommendation should not conceal the full competitive record.

### 10.0 HSE and regulatory review

HAZOP/MOC, environmental impact, fire and safety, inspection requirements, construction risk, permit and isolation requirements, statutory approvals.

### 11.0 Legal and contractual review

Contract form, warranty, liquidated damages, performance guarantee, insurance, intellectual-property terms, claims and dispute provisions, legal concurrence.

### 12.0 Schedule and milestones

Tender, engineering, manufacturing, inspection, delivery, shutdown, installation, commissioning, handover.

### 13.0 Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 14.0 Recommendation

Clear approval paragraph with amount, scope, vendor/procurement route, budget, conditions, required decision date.

### 15.0 Delegation-of-authority statement

Authority under which approval is requested, approval limit, finance concurrence, technical concurrence, escalation required.

### 16.0 Annexures

Technical evaluation, failure history, inspection report, FFS or integrity assessment, vendor quotes, financial comparison, HSE review, legal comments, budget certificate, project schedule, risk register, drawings and datasheets.

## Good vs. bad

Good: starts with clear decision, quantifies consequences, shows alternatives, reconciles cost across all annexures, states budget and authority, includes adverse information, preserves concurrence and revision history.

Bad: starts with 20 pages of background before stating the request, omits "do nothing" consequences, uses a vendor quote as financial justification without comparison, hides conditions in annexures, shows a recommended vendor without technical evaluation, does not explain authority.
