# Power Generation and Transmission

## Organisation map

| Type | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal generation | NTPC, state generation companies | Coal/gas combustion to electricity |
| Hydro generation | NHPC, SJVN, state hydro utilities | Water head drives turbines |
| Transmission | PowerGrid, state transmission utilities | High-voltage bulk transfer |
| Grid planning/regulation | CEA, CERC, state ERCs | Planning, standards, regulation |
| System operation | NLDC, RLDCs, SLDCs | Real-time grid balancing and dispatch |
| Nuclear generation | NPCIL, BHAVINI | Nuclear heat to steam/electricity under nuclear controls |

NTPC public material reports group capacity of 72,254 MW, 21,745 permanent employees and 113,355 contractual employees in FY2022–23, alongside PRADIP, CLIMS, water dashboards, renewable-asset monitoring, RPA and AI/IIoT initiatives. PowerGrid has publicly described centralised relay-database management and digitised asset management, including relay-setting control.

## Physical operations

| Asset | What it does |
|---|---|
| Coal handling plant | Receives, crushes, stores and feeds coal |
| Boiler/furnace | Burns fuel to convert water into high-pressure steam |
| Turbine | Steam expands through blades and produces rotation |
| Generator | Converts rotation into electricity |
| Condenser | Turns exhaust steam back into water |
| Cooling tower or once-through cooling | Removes waste heat |
| Ash-handling system | Collects and transports fly ash and bottom ash |
| Switchyard | Steps voltage up and connects plant to the grid |
| Transformer | Raises or lowers voltage |
| Protection relay | Detects faults and trips equipment |
| SCADA/EMS | Monitors and controls grid equipment |
| HVDC terminal | Converts AC/DC for long-distance transmission |
| Hydro dam and intake | Stores/diverts water to turbine |
| Governor and excitation system | Controls turbine speed, voltage and generator stability |

## Departments

Generation operations, coal/fuel management, boiler and turbine maintenance, electrical maintenance, C&I/automation, chemistry and water systems, civil and dam safety, grid operations and system control, protection and telecom, planning and commercial, PPA/market operations, HSE and environment, procurement, finance, HR, legal, IT/OT cybersecurity.

## Workforce and shifts

- Plant control-room operators work rotating shifts
- Field operators, chemistry, fire and emergency teams operate round the clock
- Engineers, finance, HR and planning work mainly day shifts
- Contractors perform coal handling, ash handling, maintenance, civil works, housekeeping, security and outage work
- Transmission control centres operate 24/7 with shift engineers and grid controllers

## Daily workflow example: forced outage

1. Control room receives a trip in DCS/SCADA
2. Shift engineer checks alarm sequence and protection relay indication
3. Operator records event in electronic or paper logbook
4. Maintenance opens EAM notification
5. Electrical/protection engineers download relay event files
6. Historian is checked for pre-trip trends
7. Operations informs load dispatch and commercial teams
8. Maintenance, electrical and process teams inspect the equipment
9. RCA team prepares chronology and cause hypothesis
10. Outage estimate is entered into planning or availability system
11. Management receives a forced-outage report
12. Corrective work order and action tracker are created
