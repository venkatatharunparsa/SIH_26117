# Daily Reality

What users actually do all day — not job descriptions, but lived experience.

## Process engineer

**6:45 AM** — Arrives. Opens email. Reads shift handover. A pump tripped at 2 AM. Diesel sulphur was high at 4 AM. There is a permit on the feed exchanger.

**7:00 AM** — Opens historian. Selects tags. Tag names don't match the P&ID. Spends 15 minutes figuring out which tag is which. Exports 24 hours of data.

**7:20 AM** — Opens LIMS. Sulphur sample at 4 AM shows 18 ppm against 10 ppm specification. Deviation.

**7:40 AM** — Opens DCS alarm history. Reactor temperature dip at 3:15 AM. Opens EAM. Work order on hydrogen flow meter at 3:00 AM. Opens shift log. Operator noted "H2 flow meter acting up" but didn't raise notification.

**8:00 AM** — Opens Excel. Copies historian data. Copies lab results. Builds timeline. Creates chart.

**8:30 AM** — Morning meeting. Explains the likely cause. Promises to write a deviation note.

**9:00 AM** — Starts writing the note. Needs event summary, affected quantity, root cause, corrective actions. Opens Word. Types. Copies the chart. Realizes the chart doesn't have tag names. Fixes. Copies again.

**11:00 AM** — Sends for validation.

**2:00 PM** — Gets responses. Updates. Sends for approval.

**5:30 PM** — Approved. Saved.

**Total: 8–10 hours elapsed. 4–5 hours actual work. Finding and assembling: 60%. Actual analysis: 20%. Formatting and chasing approvals: 20%.**

## Inspection engineer

**9:00 AM** — Contractor emails a 150-page scanned inspection report. Image-only PDF.

**9:15 AM** — Opens. Starts reading. 47 thickness readings on pages 12, 15, 18, 22, 27. Starts typing into Excel.

**11:00 AM** — Still typing. Some handwritten. Some faded. Some inches, some mm.

**11:30 AM** — Opens P&ID. Verifies tags. Spends 20 minutes confirming nozzle orientation.

**12:00 PM** — Opens previous inspection report from 2019. Manually matches measurement points. Some don't match. Equipment was re-rated in 2021.

**2:00 PM** — Calculates corrosion rate for 47 points. Some negative. Flags those.

**3:00 PM** — Opens design datasheet. Finds minimum thickness. Three points below minimum.

**3:30 PM** — Starts writing recommendation note.

**5:00 PM** — Sends for review.

**Total: 1–3 working days. Finding and assembling: 50–70%.**

## Maintenance engineer

**8:00 AM** — Pump P-204A tripped again. Third time this month.

**8:15 AM** — Opens EAM. 47 work orders. Some "P-204A," some "Pump 204A," some "204A." Exports. Cleans.

**9:00 AM** — Opens historian. Pulls suction/discharge pressure, flow, motor current.

**10:00 AM** — Opens DCS alarm history. Low suction pressure. Opens email. Searches "P-204A." Finds seal thread from 6 months ago. Attachment is scanned PDF. Reads.

**11:00 AM** — Opens shared drive. Datasheet in "Pumps/Archived/2018." Seal specification. Alignment report in "Maintenance/Reports/2023." Vibration in "Reliability/Vibration/2026."

**12:00 PM** — Builds timeline in Excel.

**1:00 PM** — Calls RCA meeting.

**3:00 PM** — Checks spare parts. Seal not in stock. 12-week lead time. Raises purchase requisition.

**4:00 PM** — Starts writing RCA report.

**6:00 PM** — Still writing.

**Total: 1–5 days. Finding and assembling: 40–60%.**

## HSE officer

**9:00 AM** — MOC request: change control valve material and operating range.

**9:15 AM** — Searches P&ID. Three versions in different folders. Latest approved is Rev 6. Shared drive has Rev 5. Project folder has Rev 7 (draft).

**10:00 AM** — Searches datasheet, control narrative, operating procedure, cause-and-effect. Each in a different system.

**11:00 AM** — Searches HAZOP records. Finds open recommendations. Two previous MOCs.

**12:00 PM** — Identifies reviewers: process, operations, mechanical, electrical, instrumentation, inspection, maintenance, projects, HSE. Sends.

**1:00 PM** — Waits. Chases. Consolidates.

**3:00 PM** — Reviews technical basis.

**4:00 PM** — Prepares checklist.

**5:00 PM** — Routes for approval. Tracks signatures. Chases.

**Total: 1–10 days. Finding and assembling: 40–70%.**

## Procurement officer

**9:00 AM** — Six bids for compressor overhaul. Each 200+ pages.

**9:15 AM** — Opens first bid. Reads. Copies into Excel. Opens second bid. Reads. Copies. Opens third.

**Day 2** — Continues. Builds compliance matrix.

**Day 3** — Checks deviations. Classifies.

**Day 4** — Issues technical queries.

**Day 5–7** — Receives responses. Updates matrix. Prepares comparative statement.

**Day 8** — Prepares recommendation note.

**Total: 3–15 working days. Finding, extracting, formatting: 60–80%.**

## Shift engineer

**6:00 AM** — Verbal handover: "Nothing much happened. Permit on P-204A. Compressor running hot. Lab flagged diesel."

**6:15 AM** — Checks DCS. Historian. EAM. Permit system. LIMS. Email.

**6:30 AM** — Confirms priorities.

**During shift** — Monitors. Adjusts. Coordinates. Records.

**5:30 PM** — Prepares handover. Copies from five systems into Word. Formats.

**6:00 PM** — Sends.

**Total: 0.5–2 hours per shift. Manual copying: 30–60%.**

## Finance officer

**9:00 AM** — Opens ERP. Extracts maintenance actuals. Exports to Excel.

**10:00 AM** — Opens budget. Compares. Variance.

**11:00 AM** — Opens EAM. Downloads report. Manually reconciles.

**12:00 PM** — Matches POs, GRNs, invoices.

**2:00 PM** — Calculates. Identifies drivers.

**3:00 PM** — Sends email to maintenance asking for explanations.

**Day 2** — Receives responses. Prepares report.

**Total: 2–10 days. Finding, reconciling, formatting: 50–70%.**

## Senior manager

**10:00 AM** — ₹25-crore proposal. Approval note + 15 annexures.

**10:15 AM** — Reads note. Checks request, reason, amount, budget, vendor, date.

**11:00 AM** — Opens annexures. Technical evaluation. Failure history. Inspection. Quotes. Financial. HSE. Legal.

**12:00 PM** — Checks consistency across documents.

**1:00 PM** — Sends clarifications.

**Day 2–5** — Receives. Reviews. May return.

**Day 6** — Records approval.

**Total: 1–10 days elapsed. Reading and checking: 3–8 hours. Waiting: most elapsed time.**

## The core insight

> The highest manual burden is not the calculation. It is assembling reliable input data.
