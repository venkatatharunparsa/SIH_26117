# MRPL Workforce

## Reported workforce

MRPL's FY2024–25 annual-report disclosures report:

| Category | Approximate number |
|---|---|
| Management employees | 1,162 |
| Non-management employees / permanent workers | 1,368 |
| Total employees | 2,530 |
| Permanent workers | 1,368 |
| Non-permanent workers | 4,089 |
| Total workers reported | 5,457 |
| Male employees | 2,304 |
| Female employees | 226 |
| Employees with disabilities | 46 |
| Scheduled Caste employees | 244 |
| Scheduled Tribe employees | 101 |

The distinction between "employees" and "workers" is important. The larger worker number includes a substantial non-permanent or contractor-linked workforce and should not be interpreted as MRPL's direct payroll headcount.

The previous FY2023–24 disclosure reported 1,184 permanent employees, 1,364 permanent workers and 3,959 non-permanent workers, showing that workforce figures can change with reporting classification and contracting activity.

## Workforce categories

A refinery workforce normally combines:

- Process and chemical engineers
- Mechanical, electrical, instrumentation, civil and reliability engineers
- Control-room panel operators
- Field operators and console assistants
- Fitters, welders, machinists, electricians and instrument technicians
- Laboratory chemists and sampling personnel
- HSE, fire and emergency-response personnel
- Planners, schedulers, procurement professionals and finance staff
- IT, OT, cybersecurity and automation specialists
- Managers and senior executives
- Contract workers for maintenance, civil work, cleaning, logistics, scaffolding, insulation, painting, transportation, security, loading and turnaround execution

## Shift work

Public MRPL disclosures do not provide a complete current shift roster. For a 24-hour refinery, the operational model should be assumed to include:

- Continuous rotating shifts for field operators, panel operators, utilities, laboratory coverage, fire and emergency services, security and selected maintenance support
- Day shifts for engineering, planning, procurement, finance, HR, legal, projects and most administrative functions
- Extended or round-the-clock contractor coverage during turnarounds, construction, major maintenance and emergency work
- On-call specialists for process engineering, inspection, electrical, instrumentation, cybersecurity and management escalation

An AI workbench should therefore support both office users and shift users, including mobile or low-bandwidth access, handover workflows, shift logs and time-aware search.

## What contractors do

Contractors are likely to perform a significant portion of labour-intensive and outage-related work, including:

- Scaffolding, insulation and refractory work
- Mechanical overhauls and equipment servicing
- Welding, fabrication, piping and structural work
- Tank cleaning, inspection preparation and painting
- Civil maintenance and road or drainage work
- Housekeeping, waste handling and facility services
- Security, transport and logistics
- Product loading, unloading and terminal operations
- Turnaround work packs and specialist inspection
- Construction and commissioning for capital projects

Contractor records are operationally important: gate passes, competency certificates, safety induction, permit compliance, toolbox talks, work completion certificates, wage and labour-law records, medical fitness and incident histories.

## AI workbench relevance

The workforce shape means the AI workbench must:

- Support both day-shift and rotating-shift users
- Handle mobile and low-bandwidth access for field users
- Support shift handover and time-aware search
- Respect contractor vs. employee access boundaries
- Support training and competency records
- Handle multilingual content where relevant
