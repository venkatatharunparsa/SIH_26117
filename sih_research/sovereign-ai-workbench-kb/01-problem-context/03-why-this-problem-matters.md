# Why This Problem Matters

## The scale of the problem

Every Indian refinery, PSU, defence unit, government department, regulator, bank, heavy industry, and legal institution performs high-volume knowledge work over documents and operational data that is often too sensitive for consumer cloud AI.

Across all sectors, the same pattern appears:

- Critical knowledge is distributed across ERP, EAM, historians, DCS/SCADA, LIMS, email, shared drives, portals, and paper.
- The most valuable documents are often confidential, safety-critical, commercially sensitive, personally identifiable, or security-classified.
- Staff need AI assistance for search, comparison, calculation, drafting, and summarisation.
- Public cloud AI creates concerns about data transmission, retention, jurisdiction, model training, privileged access, cyberattack, and loss of audit control.

## The shadow AI problem

A complete ban on public AI may reduce one risk while creating another: shadow AI.

Employees still need help with:

- Summarising reports
- Comparing vendors
- Extracting tables
- Writing notes
- Translating documents
- Analysing spreadsheets
- Searching old files

If the organisation provides no approved alternative, users may:

- Use personal accounts
- Use browser-based AI
- Upload screenshots
- Copy data manually
- Store output in unmanaged folders
- Use consumer translation or OCR services
- Send confidential files through personal email

Therefore, the strategic choice is not "allow AI vs. ban AI." It is: **provide a governed local AI workbench that is useful enough to displace unsafe shadow usage.**

## The Samsung incident — a transferable lesson

Samsung employees entered sensitive source code and internal information into ChatGPT. Samsung subsequently restricted employee use of generative-AI tools.

The mechanism is directly transferable to any Indian organisation:

1. An engineer wants a fast answer.
2. The internal search system is slow or unavailable.
3. The public AI tool is convenient.
4. The user uploads source code, a report, a table, or a meeting transcript.
5. The organisation loses control of the information.

## Risks for an Indian refinery or PSU

- Exposure of process bottlenecks
- Disclosure of safety-system weaknesses
- Leakage of crude or procurement strategy
- Vendor bid contamination
- Industrial espionage
- Regulatory non-compliance
- Personal-data breach
- Loss of legal privilege
- Compromise of incident investigations
- Increased cyberattack surface
- Reputational and parliamentary scrutiny
- Inability to prove who accessed or transmitted data

## Risks of not providing a sanctioned local tool

- Employees use personal accounts and personal devices
- Confidential data is uploaded to consumer services
- Output is stored in unmanaged folders
- No audit trail exists
- The organisation cannot prove data did not leak
- Regulatory and legal exposure increases

## Why now

- Open-weight large reasoning models have reached a point where a genuinely useful assistant is realistic.
- Indian GPU infrastructure is improving (IndiaAI Mission, 38,000 GPUs planned).
- Indian policy is shifting (DPDP Act 2023, CERT-In 180-day log retention, cloud sovereignty framework under discussion).
- Indian sovereign AI initiatives exist (Bhashini, AI4Bharat, BharatGen, AIKosh).
- But nothing deployable exists that industrial users can actually work with the way they use Claude or Codex.
- The gap between general-purpose models and a deployable, permission-aware, air-gapped workbench for sensitive organisational documents is the market opportunity.

## The national context

- Public and low-risk internal documents can often use approved cloud services.
- Confidential industrial, financial, legal, and personal data usually needs a controlled private or government cloud.
- Critical infrastructure, defence-classified, nuclear-security, and highly sensitive investigation data may require on-premise segmented systems or a genuine air gap.
- "Indian-hosted" is not automatically the same as "sovereign" or "air-gapped."

## The opportunity

An industrial-grade secure knowledge-work platform — not simply a local chatbot. It must provide:

- Permission-aware retrieval
- Revision control
- Entity resolution
- Calculations
- Agentic workflows
- Document generation
- Auditable approvals
