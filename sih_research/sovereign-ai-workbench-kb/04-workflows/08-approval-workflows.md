# Approval Workflows

## Capital-expenditure approval (senior manager)

Assume the proposal is for a compressor replacement or major reliability project.

### A. Package receipt

**Step 1: Receive approval package**

The manager receives a link or attachment containing: approval note, executive summary, business justification, technical report, failure history, inspection report, process impact, alternatives analysis, technical specification, vendor quotations, tender evaluation, budget availability, financial evaluation, HSE review, legal or contract comments, project schedule, risk register, draft purchase order or contract.

It may arrive through: approval workflow, email, board-management portal, document-management system, physical file for signature, or ERP workflow.

### B. Review evidence

**Step 2: Read the approval note**

The manager checks: what is being requested, why it is needed, what happens if it is not approved, amount requested, budget source, recommended vendor or procurement route, required decision date, delegation of authority.

**Step 3: Open annexures**

The manager opens: technical evaluation, failure and reliability analysis, inspection evidence, vendor quotes, financial comparison, life-cycle cost, production-loss calculation, HSE review, procurement comments, finance concurrence.

**Step 4: Check consistency**

The manager compares:

- Amount in approval note with quotation
- Scope in technical specification with tender evaluation
- Budget in finance note with ERP
- Failure consequence with production estimate
- Schedule with turnaround or production plan
- HSE controls with project method statement
- Vendor recommendation with technical score

This cross-document consistency check is one of the best AI opportunities.

**Step 5: Seek clarifications**

The manager sends questions to: process engineering, maintenance, procurement, finance, HSE, legal, project team.

Clarifications may be requested by: email, workflow comment, meeting, marked-up document, approval-system query.

The proposal may be returned for revision.

### C. Decision recording

**Step 6: Record approval**

The manager may: approve, approve with conditions, return for clarification, reject, escalate to a higher authority, request tender renegotiation.

The approval record contains: decision, date and time, authority, conditions, delegation reference, digital signature or physical signature, version of the proposal, supporting documents.

**Step 7: Create audit trail**

The record should preserve: original proposal, revisions, clarification questions, responses, reviewers, finance concurrence, technical approval, HSE approval, final decision, purchase order or project number, subsequent changes.

## Time estimate

| Activity | Typical elapsed time |
|---|---|
| Read executive package | 1–3 hours |
| Check annexures | 2–8 hours |
| Seek and receive clarifications | 1–5 days |
| Final decision | 0.5–2 hours |
| People involved | 8–20 people |

The manager's actual reading may take only a few hours, but elapsed time is often extended by clarification cycles.

## AI intervention

Read-only data: approval note, all annexures, ERP budget, purchase requisition, tender evaluation, vendor quotes, EAM failure history, inspection reports, HSE review, legal comments, prior approval precedents.

Output: Two-page decision brief, cross-document consistency report, unresolved-risk list, missing-concurrence list, option comparison, timeline and consequence analysis, draft approval conditions.

Human gate: The manager remains the decision-maker. The system should not issue approval or apply a signature.

## What takes time

- Reading long notes with incomplete summaries
- Checking whether conclusions are supported by evidence
- Comparing current proposals with previous approvals
- Finding the latest version
- Understanding commercial, technical and safety implications together
- Tracking which department has reviewed a document
- Detecting inconsistencies between the note, annexures and spreadsheets
- Requesting clarifications and waiting for revised drafts
- Preparing meeting packs
- Following up on action items

## Example

Current task: Approve a ₹25-crore compressor replacement. The senior manager may need to review failure history, production loss, reliability analysis, inspection findings, technical specification, vendor quotations, life-cycle cost, budget availability, HSE risk, alternative options, procurement recommendation, finance concurrence, legal or contract comments.

AI-assisted task: The workbench produces a two-page decision brief, a one-page financial comparison, a risk register, a list of unresolved questions and links to every source document. It must not hide adverse evidence simply to make the proposal appear stronger.

## Common approval bottlenecks

- Technical review
- Operations concurrence
- HSE review
- Finance concurrence
- Procurement check
- Legal review
- Delegated-authority approval
- Board or committee consideration

AI can prepare, route and monitor a package, but it should not blur who actually approved it.

## Auditability requirements

Users need to prove:

- Which source data was used
- Which revision was current
- Who reviewed the output
- What changed from the previous version
- Which calculations were performed
- Which assumptions were made
- Whether the final report was approved

## Good vs. bad approval note

Good: starts with a clear decision, quantifies consequences, shows alternatives, reconciles cost across all annexures, states budget and authority, includes adverse information, preserves concurrence and revision history.

Bad: starts with 20 pages of background before stating the request, omits "do nothing" consequences, uses a vendor quote as a financial justification without comparison, hides conditions in annexures, shows a recommended vendor without technical evaluation, does not explain authority.
