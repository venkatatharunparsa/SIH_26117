# International Comparison

## Region-by-region summary

| Region | Sovereignty approach | Typical secure deployment pattern | Relevance to India |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | Classified and controlled-unclassified information separated by impact levels, accreditation and network boundaries | Secure government clouds, classified enclaves, private deployments, air-gapped networks | Demonstrates that model deployment must match data classification |
| European Union | AI Act, NIS2, GDPR and emerging cloud/AI sovereignty initiatives | EU-controlled cloud, trusted infrastructure, local processing and strong auditability | Shows that provider jurisdiction and supply-chain independence matter, not just server location |
| China | Data-security, cybersecurity and generative-AI regulation under national-security and content-control frameworks | Domestic platforms, controlled data centres, local model ecosystem and strong state oversight | Illustrates national control over models, data and deployment |
| Russia | Sovereign infrastructure and domestic technology emphasis under sanctions and security constraints | Local data centres, domestic platforms and restricted external dependency | Shows resilience and supplier-independence concerns |
| Israel | Defence and critical-infrastructure security with strong cyber controls | Classified networks, defence contractors, secure enclaves and local deployment | Shows importance of cyber defence and dual-use AI validation |
| United Kingdom | Government AI playbook and a Sovereign AI Unit | Government-approved tools, risk assessment, secure public-sector environments | Demonstrates public-sector productivity AI with governance and sovereignty focus |

## Notable international references

- US GAO has warned that generative AI may aggregate unclassified information and unintentionally output classified information; it also notes that agencies must protect personal, controlled-unclassified and classified data used in AI systems.
- US NIST Generative AI Risk Management Profile provides a framework for managing risks such as data leakage, unreliable output, security, privacy and human oversight.
- European Commission's 2026 Cloud and AI Development Act proposal explicitly addresses cloud and AI sovereignty and provider independence from third countries.
- Google's Distributed Cloud Air-Gapped product illustrates the commercial direction: AI and cloud functionality can be deployed in environments with strict residency and isolation requirements.

## What this means for India

International examples support the product thesis, but they do not imply that one architecture fits every Indian customer.

The defensible position is:

- Different data classifications require different deployment postures
- "Indian-hosted" is not automatically "sovereign" or "air-gapped"
- Provider jurisdiction, supply-chain independence and audit control matter alongside physical location
- A permission-aware, evidence-citing, workflow-integrated AI workbench that runs inside the customer's security boundary is a distinct product category
